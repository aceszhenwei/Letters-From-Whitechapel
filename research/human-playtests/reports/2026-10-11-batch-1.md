## Patterns

**Recurring** (seen in the investigation `2026-10-11-investigation-arrests`):

- **v3's belief about Jack's position stayed diffuse.** It was measured at the 436 clue decisions in this batch, with the earlier investigation's script:

  | Measure | This batch (5 games) | First two human games | Jack AI v2 / Strategic Jack (40 synthetic games) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Possible locations (median) | 59 | 67 | 43 / 36 |
  | Jack in the AI's top 10 | 17% | 12% | about 40% |
  | Jack's rank among them (median share) | 0.66 | 0.64 | 0.53 / 0.59 |
  | Walks from the nearest policeman (median) | 2 | 2 | 2 |

  - **Same reading as before:** the police stood as close as against the computer's Jacks, but believed less precisely.
  - **Per game:** the top-10 share ranged from 10% to 27%, and the median number of possible locations from 45 to 100.
- **Every coach on the first night.** All five games used all 3 night-1 coaches, as both earlier games did. Coaches on later nights were nearly always spent too: allowance 2, 2, 1; used 2, 2, 1 in three games.
- **Jack was almost never within arrest reach outside the double event.**
  - **Within reach:** he was arrestable at only 2 of 436 clue decisions, both on a night-3 crime scene before his first move.
  - **Above the threshold:** v3 had an arrestable circle at belief ≥ 0.2 five times, four of them those 0.5 crime-scene coin flips.

**New patterns to watch:**

- **Double-event coin flips.**
  - **What happened:** in four of the five games a policeman reached a crime scene on night 3 before Jack moved. Each time v3 arrested at one of the two scenes, at belief 0.5 each (three by its threshold, one forced).
  - **Result:** right once, which won `g4e86b6b6af7cc2e1`, and missed three times. That is consistent with a fair coin over four tries.
  - **Verdict:** sound by v3's rule, and the only arrests that ever hit in human games so far.
- **A last-night murder next to home got through** (`g6b7af2a30b071ae6`, above). One game.
- **Hideouts repeat.**
  - **The repeats:** 126 in `g4e86…` and `g6b7…`; 141 in `g98c…` and in the first human game `g91d4014f17ae09e6`.
  - **What it suggests:** the seven games may come from very few people. That matters for every pattern above (see Limitations).

## Defect candidates

None.

- **Every arrest matches v3's code.** Each of the 7 arrests was either above its 0.2 threshold or a forced arrest (`police.js`, `clues()`: a policeman with nothing left to search must arrest).
  - **An apparent disagreement, explained:** the earlier investigation's measurement modelled only the threshold. Its script therefore reports one "disagreement" in each of `g98c…` and `gcdf…`; both are forced arrests.
- **No sign of hidden information.** Jack's true position always had a non-zero belief (0 of 436 decisions excluded him).
- **The night-4 escape in `g6b7…` doesn't look like an obvious missed move.**
  - **Why:** with 8 hideouts nearly equally likely, five policemen can't block every one-walk escape.
  - **Still open:** whether v3's containment saw this one and ranked it below others is for an investigation, not triage.

## Recommendation

**An investigation, which now meets its trigger:** a pattern to watch has recurred across batches. "v3's belief stays diffuse against human Jacks" appeared in the first two games and again here, in 5 more. Scope it as the earlier report proposed, plus the new case:

1. **The coach question:** do early coaches cause the diffuse belief? Test it with a targeted simulation of a scripted, coach-heavy Jack against Detective AI v3, compared with the same Jack spending his coaches later.
2. **The night-4 containment question:** replay `g6b7af2a30b071ae6` to night 3's end and night 4's start, and ask v3's containment (`js/ai/containment.js`) which threats it ranked and covered. Was 142 → 126 among them?

It proposes, and changes no AI. A cumulative review isn't due yet (7 games in total).

## Limitations

- **Five games, one cohort,** all Human Jack against Detective AI v3 (Normal). The detectives' role and other levels are untested by people.
- **Players are unknown and probably few.** The repeated hideouts (126 twice; 141 in this batch and in the first game) suggest the same person played several of these games. Nothing here can be counted as evidence "across several players". Experience isn't recorded either.
- **Online submissions are anonymous.** A replay proves the moves are legal and consistent, not that a person made them. Nothing in these five looked fabricated: no copied games, and different hideouts and routes.
- **Measurement limits:**
  - the belief measures are taken at clue decisions only;
  - the synthetic comparison is the earlier investigation's (40 games, seeds 611001–611020), not re-run.
- **Describe, don't generalise:** Jack's 4 wins in 5 says nothing yet about human against AI strength.
